Basic algebra · Lesson 1.1

Variables and expressions

By the end you can read an algebraic expression aloud, say what each letter stands for, and work out its value for any number.

1.A letter that stands for a number

A variable is a letter that stands for a number we do not know yet, or for a number that can change. If a cinema ticket costs 7 and n friends go, the tickets cost 7n in total, whatever n turns out to be.

Writing a number next to a letter means multiplying them: 7n is 7 × n. The number in front is the coefficient.

Why letters? A letter lets one line describe every case at once. 7n is the cost for two friends, for ten, and for any number of friends.

2.Expressions

An expression combines numbers and variables with operations, and it has no equals sign. 3x + 5 is an expression: three times a number, plus five.

The parts joined by + or − are its terms. 3x + 5 has two terms: 3x, which depends on x, and 5, a constant that does not.

3.Working out a value

To evaluate an expression, put a number in place of the variable and do the arithmetic, multiplication before addition. For 3x + 5 with x = 4:

  1. Replace x by 4: 3 × 4 + 5
  2. Multiply first: 12 + 5
  3. Then add: 17

What is the value of 2a + 3 when a = 4?

Replace a by 4 to get 2 × 4 + 3, which is 8 + 3 = 11.

4.Check yourself

In the expression 5y − 2, what is the coefficient of y?

The coefficient is the number that multiplies the variable: 5. The −2 is a constant term.

Which of these is an expression, not an equation?

An expression has no equals sign. 4x − 1 describes a value; the other two state that two things are equal.
