Graphing a line

By the end you can draw the graph of an equation like y = 2x + 1, and read its slope and its intercept straight from the equation.

1.From an equation to points

The equation y = 2x + 1 links two variables: pick any x, and it tells you y. Each pair of values is a point (x, y) on a grid. Start with a table:

x−2−10123
y = 2x + 1−3−11357

Plot the points and they fall on one straight line. That is why an equation like this is called linear.

2.Slope and intercept

Two numbers in the equation describe the whole line. In y = mx + b:

  • The slope m is how much y goes up when x goes up by 1. For y = 2x + 1 it is 2: one step across, two steps up, as the dashed steps show. A negative slope makes the line go down from left to right.
  • The intercept b is the value of y when x = 0: where the line crosses the y-axis. Here it is 1.
A shortcut: to draw y = mx + b, mark the point (0, b), then take one step right and m steps up to find a second point, and join them.

3.Check yourself

What is the slope of the line y = 3x − 2?

In y = mx + b the slope is the coefficient of x, so m = 3. The −2 is the intercept.

Where does the line y = 5x − 4 cross the y-axis? Give the value of y.

On the y-axis x = 0, so y = 5 × 0 − 4 = −4. It is the intercept, b.

A line goes down from left to right. What can you say about its slope?

Going right, y gets smaller, so each step across brings a step down: the slope is negative.
